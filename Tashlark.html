01 · The product

What Tashlark is

Tashlark (from taash, Hindi for playing cards) is an offline Android app that bundles four South Asian trick-taking card games: Call Break, 29, Court Piece and Mendikot. You play against three computer players who think about the cards they can see, never the ones they can't. There is no account, no server, no coins, chips or betting. It earns from considerate ads between matches and a one-time "Remove ads" purchase.

You always sit at the bottom of the table. The computer players are Asha, Ravi and Meera; in the three partnership games Ravi sits opposite and is your partner. Seating is drawn anticlockwise by default (the traditional direction), with a clockwise option. Matches save after every single card, so closing the app, a phone call or the system killing the app never loses a game.

Call Break

Bid your tricks, spades always trump.

  • Four players, each for themselves. All 52 cards, 13 each. Spades are always trump.
  • Everyone bids how many tricks they will take (1 to 13).
  • Follow suit, and beat the best card on the trick if you can. Out of the led suit? You must play a spade, higher than any spade already played if you can. No spades: play anything.
  • Make your bid: score the bid plus 0.1 per extra trick (bid 3, take 5 = 3.2). Fall short: lose the bid.
  • Any hand without a spade is redealt.

Defaults: must-beat on · highest bid 13 (or 8) · 5 rounds (or 10) · about 12 min

29

Bid for points, hide the trump, find the pair.

  • Two partnerships, 32 cards (7 to ace). Rank in every suit: J 9 A 10 K Q 8 7. Jacks score 3, nines 2, aces and tens 1: 28 points in the deck.
  • Four cards each, then an auction from 16 to 28. Passing is final; if the first three pass, the last player must bid 16.
  • The winning bidder hides one card face down: its suit is the secret trump. Defenders may double, the bidder may redouble, then four more cards each.
  • Until trump is shown, the highest card of the led suit wins, and the bidder may not lead trump unless holding nothing else. A player who can't follow may ask to see trump; from then on trumps count, even in the trick being played, and the asker must trump if able.
  • Pair: once trump is shown, a side holding the king and queen of trump that then wins a trick moves the target by 4 (down if it is the bidders, up if it is the defenders, kept within 16 to 28).
  • Bidders reach the target: +1 game point (2 doubled, 4 redoubled); otherwise −1. If trump is never asked for, the hand is replayed.

Defaults: pair on · double/redouble on · last-trick point off · first to +6 (or −6 loses) · about 15 min

Court Piece

Call trump from five cards, go for a court. Also known as Rang or Hokm.

  • Two partnerships, all 52 cards, aces high.
  • Five cards each first. The player after the dealer is the caller and names trump from those five; then eight more each. A caller with no jack or higher in the five gets a redeal.
  • The caller leads. Follow suit if you can, otherwise play anything. You never have to trump.
  • The hand ends when a team has 7 tricks. Callers win: 1 point, or 3 for a court (the first 7 tricks). Opponents win: 2 points, or 4 for a "goon court".
  • Dealer's team wins: the deal moves on. Callers win: same dealer; after a callers' court the dealer's partner deals.

Defaults: first to 10 (or 5, 15, 20) · about 15 min

Mendikot

Capture the tens with your partner. Also spelt Mindi or Mendicot.

  • Two partnerships, 52 cards, 13 each, aces high. The team that captures three or four tens wins the hand; at two tens each, the team with seven or more tricks wins.
  • Hidden trump (band hukum, default): the player after the dealer lays one card face down and plays from the other twelve. A player who can't follow may ask to reveal it; the card goes back to its owner, its suit is trump from then on, the asker must play a trump if they have one, and trumps played earlier in that trick don't count.
  • Cut trump (cut hukum, option): no trump at first; the first card played by someone who can't follow names trump for the rest of the hand.
  • Scoring with bonuses (default): a won hand 1, a mendikot (all four tens) 2, a whitewash (all 13 tricks) 3. Without bonuses every win is 1.

Defaults: hidden trump · bonuses on · first to 5 (or 3, 7) · about 15 min

What you see in the app

Welcome
First launch only. Introduces the four games and asks two questions: how quickly the computer players should move (Relaxed, Normal, Fast) and how strong they should be (Easy, Medium, Hard). Both can be changed later.
Home
"Deal a hand": one tile per game with its tagline, players and match length, plus "Continue …" for any saved match. A banner ad may sit at the bottom.
The table
Seat chips around a central trick, a score strip, the trump badge, one line of guidance ("Your turn", "Bid more than 18, or pass", "You must follow ♥"), decision panels for bids, trump, doubling and reveal, and your hand at the bottom. The top bar has Suggest a move (a hint with a reason), and a menu with Rules, Last trick and Abandon match. No ads, ever.
Result sheet
After each hand: who scored what and why, then standings. "Next round" or, at the end, "Play again" and "Home", plus an optional "Watch a short ad for an hour without ads".
Stats
Per game: played, won, win percentage (rounded so it never shows 100% unless every match was won), current and best win streaks, and recent matches. Matches where you used hints are marked as assisted.
Settings
Theme, four-colour deck, card size, play speed, seating direction, one-tap play, reduce motion, haptics, computer skill, house rules for each game, Remove ads and Restore purchase, Clear match history, Privacy policy, Ad privacy choices (where the law requires them) and Open-source licences.

What Tashlark deliberately is not: no real money, no coins or chips, no "bet" or "win cash" language, no online multiplayer, no account, no analytics, and no sound or music at all. Card players in this genre complain loudly about rigged opponents, ad spam and noise; Tashlark's answer to each is in section 02.

Why it stays legal and family-friendly

Teen Patti and Rummy apps sit under Play's 18+ "Simulated Gambling" rating, and India's Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025, with its Rules of 2026 (in force from 1 May 2026), bans online money games. Tashlark has no stakes, no virtual currency, no prizes and no online play: it is an offline social game of skill. It also asks AdMob for ads rated Teen or lower, and gambling ad categories must be blocked in the AdMob console (section 09).

How it makes money

Three Google AdMob formats and one Play purchase. Every rule below is in app/src/main/java/com/tashlark/app/ads/AdsManager.kt, ads/AdBanner.kt, ui/AppNav.kt and ui/table/TableScreen.kt. The guiding principle: the card table is sacred.

Its height is reserved up front so nothing jumps when it loads (or doesn't, offline). Hidden on screens narrower than 320 dp. Never on the table, Settings, Welcome, Privacy or Licences. Built
InterstitialOnly when you tap on from a result sheet"Next round" counts as a round break; "Play again" and "Home" after a match count as a match break. Shown only if every condition below holds. Built

The interstitial gate

AdsManager.atBreak() shows an interstitial only if all of these are true, and then always continues to the next screen exactly once, whether or not an ad appeared:

  1. Ads are not removed, and no ad-free hour is running.
  2. Consent allows ad requests and the Mobile Ads SDK has started.
  3. An interstitial is already loaded (the app never makes you wait for one).
  4. Today is not the install day (calendar day of first launch, stored on first open).
  5. At least 2 matches have been finished in total (FREE_MATCHES = 2), across all games.
  6. Enough time has passed since the last interstitial: 3 minutes for a match break (MATCH_GAP_MS), 8 minutes for a round break (ROUND_GAP_MS). In practice a round-break ad appears at most once in a long match.

There is no interstitial on app open, resume, the back button, "Abandon match", during a deal or in the middle of a hand. Ads play muted (MobileAds.setAppMuted(true)) and are limited to content rated Teen or lower (MAX_AD_CONTENT_RATING_T).

Consent and privacy

  • Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) runs on each app start, at most once per process. It shows a consent form only where the law requires one (EEA, UK, Switzerland, US state rules).
  • If the consent check fails (offline), the app uses whatever consent was stored previously and retries on the next start.
  • When the regulations require it, Settings shows Ad privacy choices to reopen the form.
  • Players who removed ads never have any ad loaded.

Remove ads and the ad-free hour

billing/BillingManager.kt uses Play Billing 9.1 with pending purchases enabled and automatic service reconnection. It reads the price from Play, so the price is set only in Play Console. Owned purchases are acknowledged and cached in settings (so they work offline); a pending purchase shows "Purchase pending"; if Play later reports the product as not owned (a refund), ads come back.

Suggested price: ₹149 in India (about $1.99 in the US and diaspora markets). It is below the ₹199 used for Gridlark because card players in Nepal, Bangladesh and Pakistan are more price-sensitive. Assuming 0.3–0.5% of installs buy it Estimate, that adds roughly $400–800 per 100,000 installs after Play's 15% fee: welcome, but ads remain the business.

What a player actually sees Example

  1. Install day: plays four Call Break matches. Banner on Home only. No interstitials at all.
  2. Day two, first match ends: taps "Play again"; an interstitial shows (install day is over and 2+ matches are finished).
  3. Same session: a 29 match ends 12 minutes later; another interstitial. Round breaks in the first 8 minutes of that match showed nothing; one later round break could have shown a single ad, which would then have reset the clock.
  4. Next match result: the player taps "Watch a short ad for an hour without ads". For the next hour there are no banners and no interstitials.

05 · Structure

Architecture

Two Gradle modules. :engine is plain Kotlin with no Android code: the cards, the rules of all four games, scoring and the computer players. It runs and is tested on any computer. :app is the Android app: screens, the table, saving, history, ads and billing. The app depends on the engine; the engine depends on nothing.

One table for four games

Every game implements the same two interfaces, so there is one table screen and one view model, not four.

Rules<S>
Pure rules for a single hand: toAct(s) (whose turn, or −1 when the hand is over), legalActions(s), apply(s, a) returning a new state, utility(s) (score per seat, for the AI), determinize(s, viewer, rng) (hide what the viewer can't know) and winnerOf(s, trick).
CardGame<S, M>
The match around the hands: newMatch(seed), deal(match), finishHand, isMatchOver, winners, plus everything the table needs to show: table(hand) (a TableView from your seat's point of view, never revealing what you can't see), summary, standings, progress, rulesText() and explain() for hints. aiSpecial() lets a game handle bids or trump choice with its own logic.
Action
Every decision in any game is one of eight values: Play(card), Bid(n), Pass, SetTrump(card?, suit), CallReveal, Double, Redouble, NoDouble. The table UI simply looks at which ones are legal right now and shows the matching panel: a bid grid, suit buttons, a "Set trump" bar, Double/Redouble buttons, a "Show trump" button, or playable cards.

Saving by replay

A saved match is not a snapshot of the table. It is the seed plus the list of every action. Deals are generated from the seed, so replaying the actions rebuilds the exact position, down to the face-down trump. One file per game lives in the app's private files/matches/ folder.

Covered by app/src/test/.../ReplayTest.kt: the action codec round-trips every action kind, replaying a logged match rebuilds an identical state, and saved rules round-trip. Android backup includes the settings, the database and the saved matches.

06 · Inside the engine

Engine deep-dive

Everything in engine/ is deterministic, immutable and fast enough to simulate thousands of hands per second on a phone. The four games share one card model, one random-number generator, one hand sampler and one Monte-Carlo player; each game supplies its own state, rules, scoring and a fast heuristic policy.

The shared core

Card
A value class holding one Int: suit × 13 + (rank − 2), ranks 2 to 14 (ace). Spades are 0–12, hearts 13–25, diamonds 26–38, clubs 39–51. No object is allocated in the AI's hot loops, and the code is what the save file stores. spoken gives "queen of hearts" for TalkBack.
CardSet
A set of cards as a 52-bit Long mask. Size is one bitCount, membership one AND, "cards of a suit" one mask. FULL is the 52-card deck, PIQUET the 32-card deck for 29 (7 to ace).
Rng
SplitMix64: the same seed gives the same numbers on every phone and on the build server. nextInt uses rejection sampling (no modulo bias), shuffle is Fisher–Yates, fork() splits off an independent stream. mixSeed(match seed, hand number) gives each deal its own seed.
sampleHands
Deals the unseen cards to the other seats with the right counts while honouring every known void (a seat that failed to follow a suit cannot hold it). It places the most constrained cards first, prefers seats with the most room, retries up to 60 shuffles, and only as a last resort ignores voids (contradictory constraints should never happen).
MonteCarloAi
For each sampled world and each legal action, apply the action and play to the end of the hand with the game's Policy; pick the action with the highest total utility for the seat. After at least 8 worlds it stops if 900 ms have passed.
SkillWorlds per decisionHow it plays
Easy0The game's heuristic policy only: sensible habits, no look-ahead. Beatable, never silly. Call Break bids by hand count.

Provably fair deals. A match's cards depend only on its seed and the hand number. The app cannot "decide" to give the computer better cards mid-match, and the same seed and actions always rebuild the same match, which is exactly how saving works.

Call Break · engine/…/callbreak/

StateCallBreakState: four hands, dealer, bids (null until made), completed tricks, the current trick, tricks won per seat, cards played, known voids, config. Scores are kept in tenths (3.2 is stored as 32) so arithmetic stays exact.
FlowBidding goes round once from the player after the dealer: each seat bids 1 to the maximum (no passing). Then 13 tricks; the first is led by the player after the dealer, each later one by the previous winner.
Legal playsLeading: anything. Holding the led suit: follow; with must beat on, only cards higher than the current winner, unless a spade has already trumped the trick (then any card of the suit). Void: must play a spade; if a spade is winning, only higher spades; if you can't overtrump, any card. No spades: any card.
ScoringMade: bid × 10 + overtricks (tenths). Failed: −bid × 10. After the set number of rounds the highest total wins; ties share the win.
VariantsSettings: must beat when following (on), highest bid 13 or 8, rounds 5 or 10. Engine only (fixed on): must trump when void, must overtrump, redeal any hand without a spade.
DeterminizationUnseen = all 52 minus played minus own hand, dealt to the other seats at their current sizes, honouring voids.
AIBidding by simulation (callBreakSimulatedBid): sample deals, give the others a bid from their hand count, play each deal out, build the distribution of tricks this seat takes, pick the bid with the best expected score, then average with the hand count and round down (a failed bid costs the whole bid). The hand count: aces, guarded kings, queens in 3–4 card suits, short side suits when holding 3+ spades, top spades, and 0.85 for each spade beyond three. Play policy: still needing tricks, lead a master card (side suits first) or win as cheaply as is safe; once the bid is made, play low and stay out of the way.

29 · engine/…/twentynine/

StateTwentyNineState: hands, dealer, the 16 undealt cards (stock), auction fields (turn, high bid and bidder, each seat's last bid, who passed), declarer, bid and current target, the face-down indicator (kept out of the declarer's hand until revealed), trump, reveal bookkeeping (who, which trick, forced or not, who must now trump), doubling stage and multiplier, tricks and their winners, card points per team, voids, and the seat that showed a pair. The phase is derived from these fields: bidding → trump → doubling → play → over.
FlowFour cards each. Auction from the player after the dealer: pass or bid above the current high (16 to 28, or 29 with the last-trick point); a pass is final; after three passes the auction ends, and if nobody had bid the last player must bid 16. The declarer sets one of their four cards face down as trump. Doubling (if on): the defender after the declarer, then the other defender, may double; if doubled the declarer may redouble. Then the stock is dealt, four more each.
Legal playsFollow suit if able. Before the reveal the declarer may not lead a trump while holding any other suit. A player void in the led suit may CallReveal instead of playing: the indicator joins the declarer's hand, trump becomes active, and the asker must then play a trump if they hold one. Trump counts for the trick already in progress (the winner is judged when the trick completes). A declarer left with only the indicator must reveal.
ScoringCard points J 3, 9 2, A 1, 10 1 (28), plus 1 for the last trick if enabled. Pair: after each trick once trump is shown, if the trick winner or their partner holds king and queen of trump, the target moves −4 (bidders) or +4 (defenders), clamped to 16..max, once per hand. Bidders reaching the target: +1 game point × multiplier (1, 2 or 4); otherwise −1 × multiplier. Defenders' totals never change. A hand where trump was never revealed by choice is annulled: no score, same dealer, next seed. Match: first team to +6 wins, a team at −6 loses.
VariantsSettings: pair (on), double and redouble (on), last-trick point (off). Engine only: declarer can't lead trump before the reveal (on), must trump after asking (on), match target 6.
DeterminizationThe declarer knows the indicator; everyone knows it after the reveal (and, if unplayed, it stays pinned in the declarer's hand). For anyone else before the reveal, a fresh indicator is drawn from the unseen cards (exempt from the declarer's voids) and trump becomes its suit. While the stock is undealt, the stock is re-shuffled too. A player who asked for trump and then didn't trump is recorded as void in trump.
AIBidding and trump choice use the policy at every skill (aiSpecial); doubling and card play use the Monte-Carlo search at Medium and Hard. Bid estimate from four cards: 10 + 2.5 per jack + 2 per nine with its jack (1 without) + 1 per ace + 0.5 per ten + 1.5 per extra card in the longest suit; bid the minimum legal amount if it does not exceed the estimate (2 lower if the partner already leads the auction), otherwise pass. Trump: the suit with the best length-and-points score, hiding its lowest card. Doubling: with two or more jacks. Play: lead masters that carry points; ask for trump when void and the other side is winning points; feed points to a partner who is surely winning; otherwise win cheaply or throw the least valuable card.

Court Piece · engine/…/courtpiece/

StateCourtPieceState: hands (five each while trump is chosen), the eight cards each seat will receive afterwards (stock, unknown to everyone), dealer, trump, tricks, tricks per team, the double-sir pile and streak seat, cards played, voids, config, and how many weak-caller redeals happened.
FlowThe caller (player after the dealer) names a suit with SetTrump(null, suit); no card is shown. Everyone then gets their eight more cards and the caller leads. If the caller's five are all below a jack, the same dealer redeals from the next shuffle of the same seeded stream, so replays stay identical.

ScoringSingle sir (default): the hand ends when a team has 7 tricks. Callers win 1, or 3 for a court (7–0); opponents win 2, or 4 for a goon court. Next dealer: dealer's team won → the deal passes on; callers won → same dealer; callers' court → dealer's partner. First to the target (10) wins the match.

Engine only, not yet in Settings: double sir, where won tricks pile up and a team collects the pile only when the same player wins two tricks in a row (the first collection needs at least three tricks; the last trick takes what is left; all 13 are played, 7 collected wins), and the weak-caller redeal switch. Mendikot · engine/…/mendikot/

StateMendikotState: hands, dealer, the face-down trumpCard (band hukum), trump suit, whether it is active, trumpFrom (in the current trick, trump-suit cards before this index don't count as trump), the seat that just asked for the reveal, tricks and their winners, played cards, voids and config. The setter (player after the dealer) leads the first trick in both modes.
FlowBand hukum: the setter chooses any of their 13 cards to lay face down and plays from the other twelve. Cut hukum: no trump phase. Then 13 tricks.
Legal playsFollow suit if able, otherwise any card. Band hukum: a void player may CallReveal; the hidden card returns to the setter, trump becomes active with trumpFrom set to the current position, and the asker must play a trump if they hold one. A setter whose only card is the hidden one must reveal. Cut hukum: the first card played by a void player names trump, counting from that card onwards.
ScoringThree or four tens wins; at two each, seven or more tricks wins. With bonuses: win 1, mendikot (all four tens) 2, whitewash (all 13 tricks) 3; without, every win is 1. Next dealer (as on pagat.com): dealer's team won → next player; dealer's team whitewashed → dealer's partner; otherwise the same dealer. First to the target (5) wins.
VariantsSettings: hidden trump (band) or cut trump, bonus scoring (on), points to win (3, 5, 7).
DeterminizationFor anyone but the setter before the reveal, the hidden card is unknown: the sampler deals one extra card into an empty slot of the viewer's own (with no void restrictions) so it is consistent with everyone else's voids, and trump becomes its suit. After the reveal the card is public and, if unplayed, pinned to the setter's hand.
AITrump choice: the longest suit (ties: the stronger), hiding its lowest card that isn't a ten. Lead: masters in side suits whose ten is still out (to flush the tens), then aces, otherwise low from the longest side suit; never an unprotected ten. Follow: ask for trump when void and a ten is on an opponent-won trick; in cut hukum, a void player picks the suit to make trump; drop a ten on a trick the partner is sure to win; win tens cheaply and safely; otherwise discard low from the shortest suit, keeping tens, trumps and honours.

07 · How it looks and feels

Design system: kora & neel

Card apps in this category look alike: green felt, gold gradients, glossy chips, loud casino sounds. Tashlark goes the other way: undyed khadi cloth by day, indigo at night, block-print restraint, and cards drawn to be read on a 720p phone.

Cards

  • Drawn in code (ui/cards/CardArt.kt): suit shapes are vector paths, not emoji or font glyphs, so they are crisp everywhere. One large centre pip on number cards; court cards sit in a round frame with Tiro letters; a condensed Anek index in each corner.
  • Two- or four-colour deck (Settings): four-colour makes diamonds blue and clubs green, which helps colour-blind players and small screens.
  • Two-row hand: when 13 cards can't each show a 44 dp sliver in one row, the hand splits at a suit boundary into two rows, the back row raised so every index stays visible and tappable. Playable cards lift 8 dp on your turn; others dim; the selected card lifts 20 dp with a haldi edge.
  • Two taps to play by default (select, then confirm), so a slip never plays a card. "One-tap play" is a setting.
  • Text layouts for card corners are measured once per card size and shared, so a full hand redraws cheaply on low-end phones.

Motion, feedback and accessibility

  • Played cards travel from the player's side in 260 ms on an emphasised curve; a finished trick is held, the winning card outlined, then gathered towards the winner. Reduce motion swaps travel for quick fades but keeps the pauses you need to read the trick.
  • Computer players think during a natural pause (about half a second for a card, about a second for a bid; Relaxed / Normal / Fast speed), so slow phones don't add extra waiting.
  • Haptics use Android's semantic constants: a tick when a card lands, confirm when your side takes a trick, reject for an illegal tap. There are no sounds or music at all.
  • TalkBack reads every card ("queen of hearts, playable"), names who played each table card, and announces prompts and rule problems ("You must follow ♥"). Touch targets are at least 48 dp; layouts survive 200% font size; a Large card size is in Settings.
  • Fonts: Anek for UI and indices and Tiro Devanagari Hindi for titles, both SIL Open Font License, bundled for offline use and ready for Hindi, Nepali and Bengali text later.

What we deliberately avoided

No felt, chips, coins, gold gradients, jackpots or "bet" wording (also keeps the 3+ rating). No emoji, no glassmorphism, no stock icon set (icons are hand-drawn 24 dp line icons). No ads on the table. No timers on your turn.

08 · Without Android Studio

Build and test with GitHub Actions

Every push builds the app on GitHub's servers, runs all tests and lint, and gives you an APK to install on your phone.

  1. Create an empty repository on github.com (for example tashlark), with no README.
  2. In Git Bash. The Tashlark folder needs its own repository because your home folder is already one: 
    cd ~/Desktop/Dev/Tashlark
git init -b main        # skip if already done
git add -A
git commit -m "Tashlark"
git remote add origin https://github.com/YOUR_NAME/tashlark.git
git push -u origin main
  3. Open the repository's Actions tab. The "Android build" workflow (.github/workflows/android.yml) takes about 10–15 minutes the first time.
  4. Open the finished run and download the artifact tashlark-debug-apk. Unzip it on your phone and open the .apk. Allow "install unknown apps" for your file manager or browser when asked.
  5. The debug app is called Tashlark too but installs as com.tashlark.app.debug, so it can sit next to the store version later. It always shows Google's test ads.

Pushes to main also produce tashlark-release (release APK, Play bundle .aab and R8 mapping). Without a keystore secret it is signed with a throwaway debug key: fine for testing, not for Play. A reports artifact holds test and lint reports if anything fails.

Locally on this PC: ~/.android-toolchain/g.sh :engine:test :app:assembleDebug (Java here can't reach services.gradle.org, so the local Gradle is used instead of ./gradlew). Run one build at a time; the PC is short on RAM.

09 · From APK to Play Store

Going live (India)

  1. Play Console account: one-time US$25 fee; complete developer identity verification. New personal accounts must run a closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 days before production Verify in Play Console.
  2. Release keystore (keep it and its passwords forever; you can't update the app without it): 
    keytool -genkeypair -v -keystore release.jks -alias tashlark -keyalg RSA -keysize 4096 -validity 10000
# Base64 for GitHub (PowerShell):
[Convert]::ToBase64String([IO.File]::ReadAllBytes("release.jks")) | Set-Clipboard
    Add repository secrets: KEYSTORE_BASE64, KEYSTORE_PASSWORD, KEY_ALIAS, KEY_PASSWORD.
  3. AdMob: create the app and three ad units (banner, interstitial, rewarded). Add secrets ADMOB_APP_ID, ADMOB_BANNER_ID, ADMOB_INTERSTITIAL_ID, ADMOB_REWARDED_ID. In AdMob's blocking controls, block gambling and betting categories: the app limits ads to the PG rating, but category blocking is a console setting. Publish app-ads.txt on your developer website (a USERNAME.github.io site works; template in docs/app-ads.txt).
  4. Remove ads: in Play Console create a one-time product with id remove_ads (suggested ₹149 / US$2.99).
  5. Privacy policy: publish docs/privacy.md (GitHub Pages), set secrets PRIVACY_POLICY_URL and SUPPORT_EMAIL (the in-app policy shows that address).
  6. Content rating (IARC): answer "no" to gambling, simulated gambling and real-money items. Target audience 13+ (not Families). Ads: yes.
  7. Data safety: declare the AdMob SDK's collection (device or other IDs, approximate location from IP, app interactions, diagnostics; shared with Google for advertising). No data collected by you.
  8. Payments: payments profile with your PAN (lower withholding tax) and bank account for both Play and AdMob.
  9. Store bundle: push a tag such as git tag v1.0.0 && git push --tags. On tags the build refuses to run unless the real AdMob IDs, keystore and support email are set, so a test-ads or debug-signed bundle can never reach the store. Upload the .aab from the tashlark-release artifact. Version code is the GitHub run number, so it always increases.
