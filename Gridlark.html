01 · The product

What Gridlark is

Gridlark is an offline Android app that serves four fresh logic puzzles every day: Crowns, Sun & Moon, Trail and Sudoku. Every phone gets the same puzzles on the same date, each puzzle has exactly one solution, and every one can be solved by reasoning alone, with no guessing. It needs no account and no server. It earns from considerate ads and a one-time "Remove ads" purchase.

The daily set is numbered like a newspaper issue: issue No. 1 was 1 October 2026, so today is No. 14. Difficulty follows the week: Gentle on Monday and Tuesday, Steady on Wednesday and Thursday, Tricky from Friday to Sunday. A Practice mode offers unlimited puzzles at Gentle, Steady, Tricky or Fiendish. There is also an Archive of past dailies and a Stats tab with streaks, best and median times, and an activity heatmap.

Crowns

Place one crown in every row, every column and every coloured region. Crowns may not touch, not even diagonally. Tap cycles a cell through blank, ×, crown; drag to cross out many cells at once.

6×6 Gentle · 7×7 Steady · 8×8 Tricky · 9×9 Fiendish

Sun & Moon

Fill every cell with a sun or a moon. Each row and column holds equal numbers of each, and no more than two of the same symbol sit side by side. "=" joins matching neighbours; "×" joins opposites.

6×6 Gentle and Steady · 8×8 Tricky · 10×10 Fiendish

Trail

Draw one continuous line that visits every cell exactly once. It starts at 1, passes the numbers in order and ends on the last one. Lines cannot cross walls. Drag to draw, drag back to retract.

5×5 Gentle · 6×6 Steady · 7×7 Tricky · 8×8 Fiendish

Sudoku

Every digit once per row, column and box. Gentle days use a quick 6×6 board with 2×3 boxes; everything else is classic 9×9. Pencil notes and an auto-notes button are included.

6×6 Gentle · 9×9 Steady, Tricky, Fiendish

The four small boards are drawn for this guide to show what each game looks like. They are not output from the app's generator.

Trademark note: the app deliberately uses its own names. LinkedIn has filed TANGO (USPTO 98778548) and ZIP (99060408), so the Sun & Moon and Trail names stay generic, and the store listing never mentions LinkedIn.

02 · The decision

Why this app, out of all the candidates

The earlier plan (Android App Plan 2026-27.html) listed several ideas. This one, called "Daily Grid" there, won on expected two-year ad revenue. The reasons:

  • Tier-1 demand already exists. LinkedIn and NYT daily puzzles have taught millions of US, UK and EU players to look for a short daily logic fix. Those players see ads worth 10 to 25 times more than Indian traffic.
  • Daily habits retain. LinkedIn reports that about 80% of people who play one of its games come back the next day. Daily puzzles give a reason to open the app every morning.
  • No Android brand owns the multi-type daily logic niche. The existing apps each clone one game, and most are ad-heavy.
  • Zero server cost. Puzzles are generated on the phone from the date, so there is nothing to host and nothing to break.
  • Low policy risk. No user content, no real-money mechanics, no account data.

Market context

  • Puzzle is 12.7% of Google Play game downloads (42matters).
  • Puzzle revenue is about $10B and growing about 14% a year (Naavik).
  • India is about 22% of Play downloads (Sensor Tower India Q1 2026), but rewarded eCPM there is about $0.85–1.20, against $13–30 in the US (playio 2026 benchmarks). One Indian rewarded impression earns roughly 1/10 to 1/25 of a US one.
  • eCPMs rise 20–40% in Q4 (holiday ad budgets), then dip in January (GameAnalytics 2026, playio).

03 · Revenue

How it makes money

Three sources: interstitial ads between puzzles, banner ads on the browsing screens, and rewarded ads for extra hints. A single "Remove ads" purchase switches the first two off. The rules are written to protect the daily habit, because a player who leaves takes all future impressions with them.

Placement rules, as coded in ads/AdsManager.kt

FormatWhere and whenLimits
Puzzle boardNever. No ad of any kind while a puzzle is on screen.n/a
InterstitialOnly after the player taps Next or Done on a result card (afterResult).SOLVES_PER_INTERSTITIAL = 2; MIN_GAP_MS = 4 minutes; never on install day; skipped when a review prompt is due.
BannerAnchored adaptive banner under the Today, Archive and Stats tabs (ui/AppNav.kt). Its height is reserved so content never jumps.Hidden after Remove ads, and until consent allows ads.
RewardedPlayer chooses "Watch ad" for an extra hint after the free one.FREE_HINTS_PER_DAY = 1. If no ad is ready (offline), the hint is free.
Remove adsOne-time Play purchase, product id remove_ads (INAPP). Restorable from Settings.No subscriptions. Owners get unlimited hints.

Consent comes first. On every start, gatherConsent() asks Google's User Messaging Platform whether a consent form is needed (GDPR in the EEA/UK, US state privacy laws). The Mobile Ads SDK starts only when canRequestAds() is true. Ad content is capped at rating T (teen), matching the 13+ audience. Settings shows "Ad privacy choices" wherever the law requires it.

A player who solves all four dailies triggers at most two interstitials that day, because of the one-per-two-solves cap. Their value depends almost entirely on country:

Later: mediation Planned

At launch, all ads come from AdMob alone. Once daily users are in the thousands, add AdMob mediation with InMobi, AppLovin and Meta Audience Network. More bidders on each impression usually lift eCPM, especially in India where AdMob fill alone is weaker. Mediation is configured in the AdMob console plus one adapter dependency per network, so it is a small code change.

05 · Structure

Architecture

Two Gradle modules. :engine is plain Kotlin with no Android code: it makes and checks puzzles, and it can be tested on any computer. :app is the Android app: screens, saving, ads, billing and reminders. The app depends on the engine; the engine depends on nothing.

What happens when you tap a cell

Saving is debounced: a burst of taps causes one database write, 400 ms after the last one. When the app goes to the background, saveNow() writes immediately on the app-wide scope, so the write finishes even if Android closes the screen. A mutex stops two saves from overlapping. A solved puzzle is never overwritten.

Why every phone gets the same daily puzzle

Never change after release: Daily.SEED_VERSION, each GameType.seedSalt and key, Daily.EPOCH, the Rng algorithm, or the generators' order of random calls. Any of these changes every past and future daily puzzle, and saved progress would no longer match its puzzle. Engine changes that alter output must ship behind a new seed version for new dates only.

06 · The engine

Puzzle engine deep-dive

Every generator follows the same recipe: build a random complete solution, derive clues from it, then use a solver to make sure the clues lead to that solution and no other. For Crowns, Sun & Moon and Sudoku the solver reasons like a person, in tiers, and the difficulty is the hardest tier the puzzle needs.

Crowns

  1. Random solution. Place one crown per row and column, no two touching, by randomised backtracking.
  2. Grow regions. Each crown seeds one region. Regions grow one cell at a time, each with a random speed between 0.35 and 2.25, so shapes are uneven and interesting. Region numbers are then shuffled so colours are not tied to rows.
  3. Region repair. A brute-force search looks for any other valid crown placement. If it finds one, it takes a cell where that alternative puts a crown (never a real crown cell) and moves it into a neighbouring region, keeping every region connected. This repeats until the intended solution is the only one, for at most 2n² rounds.
  4. Grade. The logic solver solves the board and reports the hardest tier used. The generator keeps trying until the tier matches the target. After 30 tries it accepts one tier either side; after 90 it accepts any solvable board, so generation always finishes quickly.
TierTechniqueUsed for
1Singles: a row, column or region with one free cellGentle (6×6) targets tiers 1–2
2Confinement: a region's free cells all lie in one row or column, or the reverse
3Pigeonhole sets: k regions squeezed into k rows or columnsSteady (7×7) targets 3–4
4Shallow look-ahead: a crown here would empty some row, column or regionTricky (8×8) targets 4
5Deep look-ahead: a crown here leads to a contradiction after propagationFiendish (9×9) targets 5

Sun & Moon

A random valid grid is built by backtracking (balanced lines, never three in a row). A share of the true "=" and "×" signs is chosen (18% Gentle, 22% Steady, 16% Tricky, 12% Fiendish). Then, in random order, each given symbol is removed if the logic solver can still finish without it; after that, each sign is removed if no longer needed. Gentle boards get n/2 symbols back so the first moves are obvious.

Solver tiers: 1 signs and the no-three rule, 2 line counts (a line with half suns gets moons everywhere else), 3 one-step look-ahead. Gentle allows tiers up to 2; the others up to 3.

Trail

The solution path starts as a snake (serpentine) through the grid and is scrambled by "backbite" moves: about 60 × cells random moves, each of which keeps it a valid path through every cell. Walls are added only on edges the solution never crosses (none on Gentle, 6%, 9% and 10% of eligible edges as difficulty rises).

Numbers start dense, every 2–4 steps along the path. An exhaustive solver then counts solutions. If a second solution exists, a number is added where it first leaves the true path. Once unique, numbers are removed one at a time while the puzzle stays unique, down to a floor: n + 3 numbers on Gentle, n + 1 on Steady, as few as possible on Tricky and Fiendish.

The solver stores the grid (at most 8×8 = 64 cells) in one 64-bit number. At every step it checks for dead ends (a free cell with fewer than two free neighbours) and that every free cell is still reachable from the line's head. Each check costs a handful of machine instructions. It gives up after 150,000 nodes during generation and the generator retries with a fresh path. Trail difficulty comes from grid size, walls and how few numbers remain, not from a technique tier.

Sudoku

A random full grid (6×6 with 2×3 boxes on Gentle, 9×9 otherwise) is filled by backtracking. Cells are emptied in random order whenever the logic solver, capped at the target tier, can still solve it. Gentle and Steady then get a few clues back (n/2 on Gentle, 4 on Steady). Tricky and Fiendish try up to six grids and prefer one that truly needs the top allowed tier.

Hints

Hints live in game/Boards.kt. A hint first fixes any wrong mark (for example "This crown can't stay here."). Otherwise it reveals one correct cell, chosen to feel natural: the crown in the smallest unsolved region, the Sun & Moon cell with the most filled neighbours, the Sudoku cell with fewest candidates, or the next few steps of the Trail. The hinted cell pulses in marigold.

How the engine is tested

engine/src/test/kotlin/com/gridlark/engine/GeneratorTest.kt runs on any JVM, locally or in CI. For each game and every difficulty it generates 25 puzzles (change with -Psamples=N) and checks:

  • Valid: the stored solution obeys every rule; givens and signs agree with it.
  • Unique: a separate brute-force counter finds exactly one solution. This runs for Crowns, Trail (with a 50-million-node budget) and Sudoku at every size, and for Sun & Moon at 6×6. Larger Sun & Moon boards rely on the logic solver reaching the stored solution, which it can only do if no other solution exists.
  • Deterministic: generating again from the same seed gives an identical puzzle.
  • Crowns regions are all connected; Trail rules enforce number order; the RNG gives the same sequence twice.

The tests also print average and worst generation times. The slowest case is Fiendish Sudoku, about 0.5 s on a desktop, which is why the app always generates off the main thread. CrownsStats prints a tier histogram when run with -Pstats=1.

  • Buttons scale down slightly while pressed.

Accessibility

  • TalkBack reads every cell, for example "Row 2, column 5, coral region, crown, breaks a rule", and a double tap acts on it.
  • Rule breaks are shown by colour and by shape (an underline under a clashing crown).
  • Colour-blind-safe regions, optional patterns, light and dark themes, reduce-motion, optional timer.
  • Banner space is reserved so nothing shifts under a finger when an ad loads.

What we avoided, on purpose

Many new apps share one look: purple-blue gradients, frosted glass panels, emoji as icons, centred hero text and rounded cards everywhere. Gridlark uses none of these. There are no gradients, no glass, no emoji; layouts are left-aligned like a newspaper column; sections are separated by 1 dp hairline rules rather than boxes; icons are drawn as simple ink paths; and colour is reserved for meaning (regions, the accent, errors).

08 · Building

Build and test with GitHub Actions

You do not need Android Studio. GitHub's servers build the app every time you push, run all the tests, and hand you an APK to install. Everything below uses Git Bash on Windows.

  1. Create an empty GitHub repository

    On github.com, choose New repository. Name it gridlark, make it Private, and do not add a README, .gitignore or licence (the project already has them).

  2. Push the project

    Your home folder is itself a Git repository with no commits. Run git init inside the Gridlark folder so the project gets its own repository and nothing else from your home folder is uploaded.

    Git Bash
    cd ~/Desktop/Dev/Gridlark
git init -b main
git add .
git status            # check: no .jks, no local.properties, no build/ folders
git commit -m "Gridlark: first version"
git remote add origin https://github.com/YOUR-USERNAME/gridlark.git
git push -u origin main

    The first push opens a browser window to sign in to GitHub. .gitignore already excludes keystores (*.jks, *.keystore), local.properties and build output.

  3. Watch the build

    Open the repository's Actions tab. The workflow is .github/workflows/android.yml, named "Android build". It runs on every push to main, on tags starting with v, on pull requests, and on demand (Run workflow button). Steps:

    1. Check out the code, install JDK 21 (Temurin), set up Gradle with caching.
    2. Run :engine:test, :app:testDebugUnitTest, :app:lintDebug and :app:assembleDebug. Lint errors fail the build.
    3. If the KEYSTORE_BASE64 secret exists, decode it to a temporary release.jks.
    4. Build the release APK and the AAB, passing your secrets as environment variables. VERSION_CODE is the GitHub run number, so every build has a higher version code automatically.
    5. Upload artifacts: gridlark-debug-apk, gridlark-release (release APK, AAB and R8 mapping.txt) and reports (test and lint reports, kept 7 days).

    A full run takes several minutes. A green tick means everything passed.

  4. Download the APK

    Click the finished run, scroll to Artifacts, and download gridlark-debug-apk. GitHub gives you a .zip; unzip it to get the .apk.

  5. Install on your phone

    Copy the .apk to the phone (USB, Google Drive, or email it to yourself). Open it from the Files app. Android asks to allow installs from that source: tap Settings, turn on Allow from this source (called "Install unknown apps"), go back and tap Install. Play Protect may warn about an unknown developer; choose to install anyway. Turn the permission off again afterwards if you like.

Launch marketing plan

  1. Closed test as a community. Recruit the 12+ testers from puzzle fans, not only friends, and ask them for honest feedback in the 14 days.
  2. Reddit. A launch post on r/AndroidGaming (respect its self-promotion rules: be the developer, answer every comment) and a "made a daily logic puzzle app, every puzzle has one solution" post on r/puzzles. Lead with what players hate elsewhere: no ads on the board, no subscriptions, no guessing.
  3. Short videos. YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels titled "Can you solve today's Crowns in 60 seconds?" Screen-record the debug build, one puzzle per day, same time daily. The daily format gives you endless content.
  4. Share text. The app's Share button already posts "Gridlark No. 14 · …, solved in 4:17, no hints" with the Play link. Encourage testers to share results in group chats.
  5. Timing. Launching before November catches the Q4 eCPM rise; early players then carry into January.
